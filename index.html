THE FUNDAMENTALS

Three ideas behind flight.

These principles help explain why an aircraft wing can produce lift.

PRINCIPLE 01

Bernoulli's Principle

In a simplified model, when fluid speed increases, static pressure decreases. The airflow around a wing contributes to a pressure difference that helps produce lift.

  • Faster airflow → lower static pressure
  • Slower airflow → higher static pressure
  • Pressure difference contributes to lift

PRINCIPLE 02

Angle of Attack & Newton's Third Law

Angle of attack is the angle between the airfoil's chord line and the incoming airflow. As the angle of attack increases, the wing changes the direction of the airflow and deflects more air downward. According to Newton's Third Law, this change in the air's motion is associated with an upward reaction force on the wing, contributing to lift.

Flift -Fair

Increasing the angle of attack generally increases lift until the wing reaches its stall region.

RESULTS

What did the testing show?

Experimental findings

The results followed the expected trend: increasing camber resulted in greater measured lift. The high-cambered airfoil produced the most lift, followed by the low-cambered airfoil, while the symmetrical airfoil produced the least. The readings for all three designs were relatively stable, suggesting that the testing method produced consistent results.

Each airfoil and its stand had an initial mass of approximately 6 g. The tests were conducted at a constant 10° angle of attack so that airfoil shape was the main variable being compared.

Average measured lift
High-cambered: 1.38 g
Low-cambered: 0.94 g
Symmetrical: 0.78 g

The high-cambered airfoil produced approximately 77% more lift than the symmetrical airfoil, while the low-cambered airfoil produced approximately 21% more lift than the symmetrical design. This supports the conclusion that, under the conditions tested, greater camber increased the lift produced by the airfoil.

REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS

Where are airfoils used?

Aircraft wings

Wing profiles balance lift, drag, speed and efficiency.

Gliders

Efficient wings help gliders produce useful lift while minimizing drag.

Wind-turbine Rotors

Airfoil-shaped rotor blades generate aerodynamic forces as they rotate.
